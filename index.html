OpenAI offers monthly API credits to eligible ChatGPT Pro and Business subscribers ↗

Coverage on October 29 describes a new OpenAI offer letting eligible ChatGPT Pro and Business subscribers link an API organization and receive monthly API credits, up to $500, that expire each billing cycle.

Canada's government takes a stake in Cohere as it opens a $4.5 billion funding round ↗

Reuters reported on Thursday that the Canadian government is taking an equity stake in Toronto-based Cohere, joining a funding round led by Germany's Schwarz Group targeting about $4.5 billion. Cohere said the round has only had an initial close, and Ottawa did not disclose the size or terms of its stake.

Why it matters: A government stake signals strategic interest in a domestic AI lab; an opened round is not closed funding, and the public commitment's size is undisclosed.

Google unveils Gemini 4 Carbon, a frontier model focused on cybersecurity ↗

Google unveiled Gemini 4 Carbon on Wednesday, a frontier model aimed at coding and cybersecurity tasks such as finding and patching vulnerabilities. The company said it will reach a subset of cybersecurity defenders first through its Fairwind Program, at introductory pricing matching Gemini 4 Argon.

Why it matters: Vendor benchmark claims for a security model need outside validation, and first access for defenders does not by itself prove the safeguards work.

New York signs a law restricting AI companion features for users under 18 ↗

Governor Kathy Hochul signed a law on October 28 requiring chatbot operators to verify users' ages and barring companion-style features, such as presenting the chatbot as human or as a personal relationship, for users under 18. It takes effect on January 1, 2027.

Why it matters: A signed law sets real obligations for chatbot design in New York, but how age checks and feature limits will work in practice is still untested.

Independent report AFP · New York State

Google DeepMind announced Genie 4 on October 27 as a research preview for trusted testers. The world model generates interactive scenes in real time with native audio and dialogue, and lets users steer a running scene with plain-language prompts.

Why it matters: A preview shows where interactive simulation is heading, but access is limited and independent testing has not yet shown how well it holds up outside demonstrations.
